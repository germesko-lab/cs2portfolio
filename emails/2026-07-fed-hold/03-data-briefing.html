01 — WHAT HAPPENED

On June 17, the Federal Reserve held its target range for the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% — the fourth consecutive meeting without a change. For your savings, a long pause is a window, not a shrug: it’s time you can use.

02 — WHY IT TOUCHES YOUR CASH

The federal funds rate is what banks charge each other to borrow money overnight. It’s not your rate — but it anchors what your bank is willing to pay you on savings and CDs.

03 — OUR POSITION

A pause rewards people who act during it. When the Fed eventually moves, banks reprice quickly — and they tend to cut what they pay savers faster than they cut what they charge borrowers. The benchmark has now sat at that level for four straight meetings. If cash you plan to spend in the next few years is earning a small fraction of it, the gap isn’t the Fed’s doing. It’s your bank’s.

Check what your cash is actually earning this week. It takes five minutes.

04 — WHAT WE’RE NOT SAYING

The vote to hold was unanimous. Where rates go from here was not — policymakers are openly split on the rest of the year. We don’t make predictions, and your savings account doesn’t need one. A rate you can see today beats a forecast you can’t.
