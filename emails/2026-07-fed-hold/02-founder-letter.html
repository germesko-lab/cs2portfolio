On June 17, the Federal Reserve held its target range for the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% — the fourth consecutive meeting without a change. For your savings, a long pause is a window, not a shrug: it’s time you can use.

Quick refresher, no jargon: the federal funds rate is what banks charge each other to borrow money overnight. It’s not your rate — but it anchors what your bank is willing to pay you on savings and CDs.

Here’s my position, plainly: a pause rewards people who act during it. When the Fed eventually moves, banks reprice quickly — and they tend to cut what they pay savers faster than they cut what they charge borrowers. The benchmark has now sat at that level for four straight meetings. If cash you plan to spend in the next few years is earning a small fraction of it, the gap isn’t the Fed’s doing. It’s your bank’s.
